Welcome to the Mappy Python Diaries!#

Mappy Python Diaries is a living textbook project designed to enhance educational accessibility and interdisciplinary research in Geographic Information Science (GIS) through the use of Open Educational Resources (OER). By leveraging platforms like Jupyter Book and GitHub, the project aims to provide high-quality, cost-free, and disability-friendly educational materials that promote data fluency and interdisciplinary learning. The project demonstrates a commitment to collaboration and community engagement, actively involving undergraduate students in the process of updating educational materials. Hands-on experience in curriculum development and content creation enhances the quality of educational resources, while cultivating a culture of knowledge-sharing and mentorship within the academic community.

Check out our Github Repository for more details!

About the Authors#

Seda Şalap-Ayça, PhD#

Department of Earth, Environmental & Planetary Sciences; Institute at Brown for Environment & Society, Brown University, Providence, Rhode Island, USA
Research Profile: Researchers@Brown
ORCID iD: 0000-0002-5788-9563
LinkedIn: sedasalapayca
Email: seda_salapayca@brown.edu

Dr. Seda Şalap-Ayça is an Assistant Professor of Practice at the Brown University Department of Earth, Environmental and Planetary Sciences (DEEPS) and Institute at Brown for Environment and Society (IBES). She is a GIScientist passionate about solving spatial decision making problems and understanding the role of uncertainty in spatial models, particularly for human-environment interactions. She did her Ph.D. in Geography in a joint doctoral program between UC Santa Barbara and San Diego State University. As a Ph.D. Student, she focused on spatially explicit uncertainty and sensitivity analysis methods for land use models. As a researcher and educator at University of Massachusetts Amherst, she has taught an array of GIS courses at various levels. At Brown University, she is eager to engage with diverse minds and continue to work with her colleagues on the complexities of human-environment dynamics.
